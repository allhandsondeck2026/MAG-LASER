01 / Mission overview

Designing for a hostile
radiation environment.

Cosmic radiation is a systems problem. Mag-Laser Active Shielding & Smart Nanomaterial Hybrid Defense explores how active fields and passive materials might be combined, while making assumptions and unresolved engineering questions visible.

Layered protection, with explicit trade-offs.

The proposal combines a controllable magnetic subsystem with a plasma/gas concept and passive material layers. A supporting power and thermal architecture is included as a design workstream, not as a verified integrated system.

SUBSYSTEM 01 / ACTIVE
Charged-particle concept

Active magnetic braking

The draft proposes a coil wrapped around a layered ferromagnetic structure, using a controlled current to generate a magnetic field. A magnetic field can exert a Lorentz force on moving charged particles, but shielding effectiveness depends on particle rigidity, field geometry, and the system's scale.

Draft materialPure iron (>98%); magnetite / iron

The draft also describes copper with a silver coating and an oxide layer separated by 2 mm Kapton tape. The exact stacking order and interfaces need a single, dimensioned cross-section.

SUBSYSTEM 03 / PASSIVE
No electrical input to passive layer

Passive material shielding

This layer uses Hydrogenated BNNTs (hydrogenated boron-nitride nanotubes) as the approved, unified smart material, intended to slow and absorb neutrons. Hydrogen-rich materials can moderate neutrons, and boron can capture some thermal neutrons. It draws 0 W (passive): no electrical input is required. No test data or complete radiation transport analysis has been supplied yet.

SUBSYSTEM 04 / SUPPORT
Architecture discrepancy

Power storage & thermal insulation

The draft describes single-wall carbon nanotubes (SWCNTs) coated with conductive polymers and hybrid supercapacitors for energy storage, alongside aerogel and multilayer insulation (MLI) for thermal management. A compact Kilopower reactor appears in the newer concept, but is not technically detailed in the supplied draft.

Energy storage draftSWCNTs + hybrid supercapacitors
Thermal materialsAerogel; reflective MLI
MLI description10–40 thin layers noted

Pnet = Pgeneration − Ploads − Plosses
Power budget not yet closed.

A complete design needs load profiles, reactor mass and output assumptions, thermal rejection, storage capacity, and redundancy. No total spacecraft power claim is made here.

Magnetic radiation shielding

Hydrogen, boron, nitrogen and BNNT materials

Plasma radiation-shielding concept

NASA's archived technical report reviews a proposed plasma-based shielding idea and identifies practical engineering questions. This is historical concept research, not evidence that the proposed plasma layer is flight-ready or proven effective.

Air revitalization and resource recovery

Describes spacecraft environmental-control systems, including cabin-air cleaning, carbon-dioxide removal, oxygen generation and water recovery. NASA reports about 90% water recovery on the station; that figure is not a gas-recycling efficiency and must not be presented as one.
